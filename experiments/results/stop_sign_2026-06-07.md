# Stop-sign multi-interval R22 retrofit (2026-06-07, v0.14.0)

The v0.5.18 cross-family paraphrase-axis sweep against the stop-sign benchmark (`stop_sign_2026-05-18.md`) characterized agreement (κ_C vs analyst) for 13 models × 3 δ(ra) paraphrase variants — 39 cells in total. v0.11.0 added test-retest κ as the within-model reliability analog of κ_F\* and demonstrated it for 3 models × 1 variant against a separate 4-item paper-aligned R22 benchmark (`stop_sign_2026-06-06.md`).

The methodology paper's R22 framing treats reliability evidence as **co-equal** to agreement evidence: a single κ_C is reporting a point on an unknown distribution, and the analyst needs reliability evidence at the time scales relevant to the scope claim to interpret it as signal. v0.14.0 closes the gap left by the v0.11.0 demonstration: **every cell in the v0.5.18 cross-family sweep now has Phase 1 R22 evidence (back-to-back + 1h drift) backing it**, captured under the same `defeasible-explicit-v1` prompt and EndorsementConfig the original v0.5.18 sweep used.

Headline finding: **31 of 39 cells are perfectly stable at both intervals (κ=+1.000)**. The remaining 8 cells split into four distinct kinds of non-stable behavior, each of which says something specific about the model + variant combination. The reliability axis is not measurement noise: it tracks how committed each model is to its inferential reading of the paraphrase variant, and it correlates with the v0.5.18 paraphrase-axis disagreement signal in the predicted direction.

This analysis presents the 39-cell Phase 1 R22 results grouped by paraphrase variant, then unpacks the eight non-stable cells. The v0.5.18 paraphrase-axis findings (which models reproduce Simonelli's analyst row under which δ(ra), per `stop_sign_2026-05-18.md`'s Tables 1–3) are unchanged; this is methodological scaffolding rather than a new substantive sweep.

## Setup

- **Benchmark**: `examples/stop_sign/benchmark.json` (the paper's Example 1). n=4, m=1, paper-aligned. Analyst row: `[good, good, good, bad]`. Unchanged.
- **Paraphrase variants** (same as v0.5.18): `original` (`$a$ is red`), `intrinsic` (`a has the standard color of stop signs`), `perceptual` (`a visibly appears red`). Each variant is constructed by runtime δ(ra) substitution via `make_variant_benchmark()` from `experiments/paraphrase_axis_triangulation.py`; the variant benchmark is reused identically across all three captures of a cell.
- **R22 capture parameters**: `--n-samples 3 --temperature 0.0 --max-tokens 2048`. Verification prompt: `defeasible-explicit-v1` (the same one used in the v0.5.18 cross-family sweep, defined as `DEFEASIBLE_PROMPT` in `paraphrase_axis_triangulation.py`).
- **Intervals**: `--interval-s 0 --interval-s 3600` per cell. Three captures: baseline → back-to-back (within-session floor) → 1h-later (short-horizon across-update drift). Each cell produces a `MultiIntervalRetestResult` with two pairs.
- **Identity criterion**: declared once in `experiments/results/stop_sign/retest/claims-r22-phase1.json`, threaded into every cell's `MultiIntervalRetestResult.identity_criterion`. Asserts `same_provider_model_id`, `cross_update_identity_asserted`, `same_scaffolding` over the 1h window.
- **Capture harness**: `experiments/scripts/stop_sign_multiinterval_r22_retrofit.py`. Parallelized across cells via `concurrent.futures.ThreadPoolExecutor` (default `max_parallel=8`); 5-min heartbeats during the 3600s sleep so the harness sees periodic stdout activity. Total Phase 1 wall clock: ~5 hours across 5 batches of 8 cells.
- **Cost**: 39 cells × 3 captures × 4 items × 3 samples ≈ 1404 LLM calls. Mix of frontier + flash/mini tiers across six families.

Output per cell: `experiments/results/stop_sign/retest/<model>-<variant>/{eta-0,eta-1,eta-2}.{json,run.jsonl}` + `experiments/results/stop_sign/retest/<model>-<variant>-multi-retest.json`.

## The eight non-stable cells, by shape

The 8 non-stable cells split into **four distinct reliability shapes**, each of which says something different about the model + variant combination:

### Shape 1: Within-session burstiness that resolves at 1h (3 cells)

- **gpt-5.4-mini-original**: κ=+0.500 → +1.000
- **gpt-5.4-mini-intrinsic**: κ=+0.500 → +1.000
- **deepseek-v4-flash-perceptual**: κ=+0.500 → +1.000

The back-to-back capture catches a sampling burst; by 1h-later the per-request noise has averaged out. **All three are smaller / distilled models** (`mini`, `flash`). Their full-size siblings (gpt-5.4 full across all variants; deepseek-v4-pro on original + intrinsic) are perfectly stable at both intervals. Reading: smaller-capacity models have noisier n=3 sampling under temperature=0 even on items where the inferential commitment is clean.

### Shape 2: Persistent self-disagreement (2 cells)

- **qwen3.6-flash-perceptual**: κ=+0.500 → +0.500
- **gemini-2.5-pro-perceptual**: κ=+0.500 → +0.500

The model flips on the same item every replication regardless of time interval. Not within-session noise (would resolve) and not 1h drift (κ would change with time). This is the model **defeasibly disagreeing with itself** on one item where the inferential commitment is delicate — specifically items where the perceptual reading of `is red` is most defeasible (e.g. cells involving darkness, paint damage, or non-reflectivity defeaters). The model knows the inference is contested and won't pick a side, twice.

### Shape 3: Real short-horizon cross-update drift (1 cell)

- **deepseek-v4-pro-perceptual**: κ=+1.000 → +0.500

**The textbook R22 failure mode** — and the most methodologically significant single cell in the dataset. The within-session floor is perfect: the model reproduces its column back-to-back exactly. But 1h of elapsed wall clock was sufficient to flip a verdict. Stochastic noise can't explain this (the same model returned identical columns ~88s apart). The most parsimonious reading: OpenRouter's routing for `deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro` on the perceptual variant served a different model variant for the 1h-later capture, OR cache state shifted in a way that affected one item.

This cell's v0.5.18 κ_C of +1.000 (perfect agreement with analyst) is followed an hour later by a verdict column where one item flipped. The published agreement number doesn't characterize the model's actual practice on that cell; it characterizes one moment of it.

### Shape 4: Degenerate-but-consistent (2 cells)

- **claude-haiku-4.5-original**: κ=undefined both
- **qwen3-max-intrinsic**: κ=undefined both

The model returns the same verdict column both times — perfectly reproducible behavior — but Cohen's κ is undefined because the substantive intersection has no class variation (p_e = 1, formula's denominator is zero). For Haiku-original: the v0.5.18 analysis already flagged this cell as a perceptual-default that rejects the analyst row across all four items (all-bad). For Qwen3-max-intrinsic: two of four items flip across runs into ABSTAIN territory but the substantive items that remain are all the same class. Reliability *is* perfect; the metric just isn't well-defined on this column shape. The v0.13.0 retest-aware report's stability-verdict ladder treats `undefined` as the worst-case for the R22 audit cap, which is methodologically correct: an analyst can't claim reliability from a degenerate column even if it reproduces perfectly.

## Cross-cutting reading: reliability ≠ independent noise

Two observations dominate the data:

**First, 6 of 8 non-stable cells appear on either the perceptual variant (4 of 8) or the variant a model defaults *against* (Haiku-original and Qwen3-max-intrinsic are the cells where each model's v0.5.18 paraphrase-axis defeats the analyst row).** The paraphrase axis and the reliability axis are not independent: where models *defeasibly disagree with each other* (the perceptual reading, where defeaters like darkness or paint damage are most active), they also *defeasibly disagree with themselves*.

**Second, family-level patterns track model capacity and provider stack:**

- **GPT family**: GPT-4.1, GPT-5.4 perfectly stable everywhere. GPT-5.4-mini bursty on the 2 variants where the full model is stable (original, intrinsic) — but perfectly stable on perceptual. The mini variant has less stable n=3 sampling on the *easier* variants where the larger model commits cleanly; on the perceptual variant where the larger model also commits cleanly, the mini follows suit.
- **Claude family**: Opus 4.7 perfectly stable across all 3 variants. Haiku 4.5 perfectly stable on intrinsic + perceptual; degenerate-consistent on original (rejects analyst row reproducibly).
- **Mistral family**: Large and Small both perfectly stable across all 6 cells. The smallest fully-stable family.
- **DeepSeek family**: v4-pro stable on original + intrinsic, **substantively unstable at 1h on perceptual**. v4-flash stable on original + intrinsic, bursty on perceptual. Both DeepSeek variants concentrate their non-stability on the perceptual reading.
- **Qwen family**: 3-max stable on original + perceptual, degenerate-consistent on intrinsic. 3.6-flash stable on original + intrinsic, persistent flip on perceptual.
- **Gemini family**: 2.5-pro stable on original + intrinsic, persistent flip on perceptual. 2.5-flash perfectly stable across all 3 variants — the only "small" family member with no R22 non-stability anywhere in the sweep.

The methodology paper's R22 framing predicts this distribution: **reliability and agreement track together, not independently, because they're two views of how committed the model is to a defeasible material-inferential practice**. A high κ_C with low κ_test-retest reports a commitment the model doesn't actually have. The v0.5.18 paraphrase-axis κ_C numbers and the v0.14.0 Phase 1 R22 κ numbers form a coherent joint signal: cells where the v0.5.18 κ_C was unanimous in the model's defaults (e.g. perfect agreement on original and intrinsic for most cells) are also stable; cells where v0.5.18 surfaced inter-model disagreement (concentrated on perceptual) are exactly where Phase 1 surfaces intra-model disagreement.

## Comparison to v0.11.0 3-model demo

The v0.11.0 `stop_sign_2026-06-06.md` captured back-to-back R22 evidence for 3 models × 1 variant against a separate 4-item paper-aligned R22 benchmark. All three models hit κ=+1.000 — confirming the within-session reliability floor is essentially zero on the canonical benchmark.

The v0.14.0 Phase 1 retrofit extends that to 39 cells × 2 intervals. The within-session floor finding from v0.11.0 holds across the cross-family sweep: 32 of 39 cells (82%) hit κ=+1.000 back-to-back. The new evidence the v0.14.0 retrofit adds is the **1h drift** and the **per-cell reliability shape** — neither of which the v0.11.0 demonstration could surface with single-interval captures.

## Reading the Phase 2 evidence (forthcoming as commits to `main`)

The v0.14.0 staged-composition pattern lets Phase 2 day-out / week-out R22 evidence ship as separate `infereval retest --auto --append-to <multi.json>` invocations days or weeks after Phase 1, without the orchestrator process needing to stay alive for the elapsed window. Each Phase 2 append grows the `MultiIntervalRetestResult` from two pairs (back-to-back + 1h) to three or more pairs.

The cells worth prioritizing for Phase 2 captures, ranked by methodological yield:

1. **deepseek-v4-pro-perceptual** (1h drift): does the drift continue at day / week scales? If yes, OpenRouter's routing inside an OpenRouter→DeepSeek path is genuinely unstable. If no, the 1h flip was a one-off cache event.
2. **qwen3.6-flash-perceptual and gemini-2.5-pro-perceptual** (persistent flips at 0s+1h): does the persistent flip pattern hold at week scales? If yes, the cell's analyst-disagreement is a genuine model property, not a session artifact.
3. **gpt-5.4-mini-original and gpt-5.4-mini-intrinsic** (within-session noise resolves at 1h): does the noise resolution hold at day scales? The mini model's noise might re-emerge at longer intervals.

When Phase 2 captures land they will be summarized in a follow-up section here or in dated `stop_sign_<later-date>.md` markdowns. The R22 audit cap is computed worst-case across all captured pairs, so a Phase 2 substantively-unstable pair will downgrade the v0.13.0 `infereval report --retest` verdict for that cell.

## Reproducibility

```sh
# Set up API keys (anthropic / openai / openrouter):
export ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=...
export OPENAI_API_KEY=...
export OPENROUTER_API_KEY=...

# Phase 1 sweep (~$10-25, ~5h wall clock at default --max-parallel 8):
python experiments/scripts/stop_sign_multiinterval_r22_retrofit.py

# Subset (smoke-test or retry):
python experiments/scripts/stop_sign_multiinterval_r22_retrofit.py --only deepseek-v4-pro

# Resume after interruption (skips cells with existing multi-retest.json):
python experiments/scripts/stop_sign_multiinterval_r22_retrofit.py --skip-completed

# Dry-run (no LLM calls):
python experiments/scripts/stop_sign_multiinterval_r22_retrofit.py --dry-run
```

Phase 2 append (run days or weeks after Phase 1):

```sh
infereval retest --auto \
  --benchmark <variant-benchmark.json>  # constructed by make_variant_benchmark
  --provider openrouter --model deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro \
  --n-samples 3 --temperature 0.0 --max-tokens 2048 \
  --append-to experiments/results/stop_sign/retest/deepseek-v4-pro-perceptual-multi-retest.json
```

The append finds `eta-0.json` next to the multi.json automatically and computes `interval_s` from elapsed wall clock between the saved `eta-0.started_at` and the fresh capture's `started_at`.

## Caveats

- **Identity criterion**: the v0.14.0 Phase 1 captures assert `same_provider_model_id`, `cross_update_identity_asserted`, and `same_scaffolding` over the 1h window. The deepseek-v4-pro-perceptual 1h drift result is *evidence against* the analyst-substantiated portion of the criterion — that's the methodological point of recording the assertion. Provider APIs don't expose model-version snapshots; the analyst commits on faith.
- **m=1**: the stop-sign demonstration benchmark has a single analyst column. `κ_F*(β)` is undefined; the v0.13.0 report's R22 audit cap acts only on the worst-case across pairs.
- **n=4**: 4 items per cell makes any single flip a 25% flip rate. The κ=+0.5 cells are 1-item flips; the methodological reading of `±0.5` on n=4 is bounded — a flip out of four is a clean signal of *some* instability but says little about which kind.
- **Endorsement config matches v0.5.18 exactly** (`defeasible-explicit-v1` prompt via DEFEASIBLE_PROMPT, n_samples=3, temperature=0.0, max_tokens=2048) — the R22 retrofit is conducted under identical conditions to the original sweep so the evidence is comparable.
- **The v0.5.18 paraphrase-axis findings are unchanged.** The 11-of-13 reproduction of Simonelli's analyst row under the original δ(ra) is the paper's empirical anchor and is not retested here. This analysis adds reliability evidence per cell; it does not regenerate the agreement evidence.
- **Phase 1 is necessary but not sufficient to characterize all observed reliability behaviors.** The 1h horizon captures within-session burstiness and short-horizon cross-update drift; longer-horizon cross-update drift (the v0.10.0 Gemini-pulmonology 2.5-week scale) requires Phase 2 appends, forthcoming as commits to `main`.
